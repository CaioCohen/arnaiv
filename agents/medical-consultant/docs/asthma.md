# Asthma

Asthma is a chronic inflammatory disease of the airways. The air passages
become swollen and overly reactive, which can cause episodes of wheezing,
shortness of breath, chest tightness, and coughing. Symptoms may be mild,
intermittent, or severe.

Common symptoms:
- Wheezing
- Cough, especially at night or early morning
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness
- Worse symptoms after exercise, smoke exposure, allergens, or respiratory
  infections

Common causes and triggers:
- Allergens such as dust mites, pollen, pet dander, or mold
- Viral respiratory infections
- Exercise
- Cold air
- Smoke, pollution, strong odors, or chemical fumes
- Stress

How asthma is commonly treated:
- Quick-relief inhalers such as bronchodilators for sudden symptoms
- Controller medicines such as inhaled corticosteroids for long-term control
- Trigger avoidance
- Monitoring of symptom frequency and inhaler use
- Emergency evaluation for severe breathing difficulty, bluish lips, or trouble
  speaking in full sentences

Patients with frequent or worsening symptoms should be evaluated by a clinician
to confirm the diagnosis and adjust treatment.
