# Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, or COPD, is a long-term lung disease
that limits airflow and makes breathing difficult. It often includes chronic
bronchitis, emphysema, or both.

Common symptoms:
- Ongoing cough
- Increased mucus production
- Shortness of breath, especially with activity
- Wheezing
- Frequent respiratory infections
- Fatigue

Common causes and risk factors:
- Cigarette smoking
- Secondhand smoke exposure
- Long-term exposure to air pollution, dust, or chemical fumes
- Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency in some patients

How COPD is commonly treated:
- Smoking cessation
- Inhaled bronchodilators
- Inhaled steroids in selected patients
- Pulmonary rehabilitation
- Vaccination against influenza and pneumonia when appropriate
- Oxygen therapy in advanced cases
- Treatment of flare-ups caused by infection or irritation

Emergency evaluation is important for severe shortness of breath, confusion,
low oxygen symptoms, or a rapid worsening of breathing status.
