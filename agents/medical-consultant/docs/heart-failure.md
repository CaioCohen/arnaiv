# Heart Failure

Heart failure is a condition in which the heart does not pump blood as
effectively as the body needs. It can be chronic or can worsen suddenly.

Common symptoms:
- Shortness of breath
- Swelling of the legs, ankles, or feet
- Rapid weight gain from fluid retention
- Fatigue
- Reduced exercise tolerance
- Waking up short of breath
- Persistent cough, sometimes worse when lying down

Common causes and risk factors:
- Coronary artery disease
- Prior heart attack
- Long-standing hypertension
- Heart valve disease
- Cardiomyopathy
- Irregular heart rhythms

How heart failure is commonly treated:
- Diuretics to reduce fluid overload
- Medicines that reduce cardiac strain and improve heart function
- Sodium and fluid management when prescribed
- Monitoring daily weight and symptom changes
- Treatment of the underlying heart condition

Emergency evaluation is needed for severe breathing difficulty, chest pain,
fainting, confusion, or rapidly worsening swelling and fluid retention.
