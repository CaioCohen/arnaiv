# Urinary Tract Infections

Urinary tract infections, or UTIs, are infections that can affect the bladder,
urethra, or kidneys. Bladder infections are more common, while kidney
infections are more serious.

Common symptoms:
- Burning with urination
- Frequent urge to urinate
- Passing small amounts of urine
- Lower abdominal discomfort
- Cloudy or foul-smelling urine
- Blood in the urine
- Fever, back pain, nausea, or vomiting in kidney infections

Common causes and risk factors:
- Bacterial infection, often from intestinal bacteria
- Sexual activity
- Urinary retention
- Kidney stones
- Catheter use
- Pregnancy
- Diabetes

How UTIs are commonly treated:
- Antibiotics selected based on the suspected infection and patient factors
- Hydration
- Pain relief measures when appropriate
- Further evaluation for recurrent infections, kidney involvement, or
  complicating factors

Prompt clinical assessment is important for fever, flank pain, vomiting,
pregnancy, male patients with suspected UTI, or symptoms of sepsis.
